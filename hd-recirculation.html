📖 About Recirculation

References: Salimi J, Razeghi E, Karjalian H, et al. "Predicting hemodialysis access failure with the measurement of dialysis access recirculation." Saudi J Kidney Dis Transpl 2008;19(5):781-4 (PMID 18711295). · Schneditz D et al. Kidney Int 1992;42(6):1450-6 (PMID 1474779).

What it measures: The fraction of dialyzed blood that returns to the dialyzer without passing through the systemic circulation — a marker of vascular access dysfunction.

Clinical significance:

  • < 10%: Normal recirculation
  • 10-15%: Borderline — investigate access
  • > 15%: Significant recirculation — access dysfunction likely

Three-Needle Method: Uses peripheral (P), arterial (A), and venous (V) urea samples. Most accurate, requires a peripheral draw.

Two-Needle (Stop-Flow) Method: Draw arterial (red port) and venous (blue port) samples. Decrease BFR to 50 mL/hr, clamp the venous line (closest to patient), allow venous pressure to rise until the machine alarms, then draw from the arterial port (label as PERIPHERAL). Uses the same formula: (P − A) / (P − V) × 100.
