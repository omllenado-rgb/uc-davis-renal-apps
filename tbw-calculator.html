Watson Formula:
Male: TBW = 2.447 - (0.09156 × age) + (0.1074 × height) + (0.3362 × weight)
Female: TBW = -2.097 + (0.1069 × height) + (0.2466 × weight)

📖 About the Watson Formula

Source: Watson PE, Watson ID, Batt RD. "Total body water volumes for adult males and females estimated from simple anthropometric measurements." Am J Clin Nutr 1980;33(1):27-39.

What it estimates: Total body water (TBW) in liters, derived from age, height, and weight. It is the most widely used anthropometric method for estimating TBW in clinical practice.

Clinical use in dialysis:

  • Estimates the volume of distribution (V) for urea — used in Kt/V adequacy calculations
  • Guides dry weight assessment and fluid management
  • Helps interpret bioimpedance and clinical volume status

Limitations: Developed in healthy adults; may overestimate TBW in obese patients and underestimate in the elderly or edematous states. Not validated for pediatric use.
